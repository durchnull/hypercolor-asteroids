# Code of conduct

This cabinet is shared, and the game on it is hostile on purpose. You write
events that ruin other people's evenings. You make the kraken meaner the week
somebody said it was hard enough. The rules keep a public tally of who has bent
what, and your own ambushes start coming for you sooner when you do.

None of that is permission to be unpleasant to a person. The line runs exactly
where you would expect it to:

> **Aim it at the ship. Never at the pilot.**

## Welcome here

- Events, ambushes, and difficulty nobody asked for. That is the game, and the
  person who never sees their own event fire is you.
- Saying a change made the game worse, and saying why. GR8 is on your honour
  and it only works if somebody is willing to say it out loud.
- Arguing with the rules — in the open, in their own commit, with a
  `Rule-Change:` line (GR10). Every rule in this file's neighbour was somebody's
  argument that won.
- Landing something rough, and finding out from the room that it was rough.

## Not welcome here

- Harassment: slurs, sexual attention nobody invited, threats, or continuing to
  contact somebody who has asked you to stop. This holds whether it happens in
  a pull request or somewhere it followed them from one.
- Personal attacks, including one wearing the costume of feedback on the work.
  "This wave is unfair and here is the timing" is review. "Only an idiot would
  ship this" is not, and the second one is not improved by being about code.
- Publishing somebody's private information.
- Anything directed at a person for who they are rather than for what they
  landed.

One thing that looks like an exception and is not. An event file may carry a
**dedication** — an event addressed to a named pilot, which the game prints under
the banner when it fires. That is the single place a person's name belongs on
an ambush. It is addressed to them, not aimed at them, and it stops being fine
the moment it is about anything other than flying.

## Where this applies

Anywhere the project happens: pull requests, issues, commit messages, branch
names, the chronicle, and the prose inside the files. A commit message is
permanent here in a way a chat line is not — the book quotes it back a year
later, under your name.

## If something goes wrong

Mail **david@durchnull.de**, who owns the cabinet. Reports stay private, you
will get an answer, and you do not have to be the person it happened to in
order to send one.

Do not use a public issue for this. Issues are the right place for almost
everything else in this project and the wrong place for this.

## What happens then

Roughly in order, and the owner decides which rung fits:

1. **A word about it,** privately, saying what crossed the line. Most things
   stop here, and most things that reach this point were careless rather than
   meant.
2. **A warning,** with the specific behaviour named and what happens if it
   continues.
3. **A pause** — no pull requests, no issues, for a stated period.
4. **Out,** permanently.

Landed work is not reverted as a punishment. The history is the scoreboard and
rewriting it to erase somebody is its own kind of dishonesty; if a change has
to come out, it comes out as a change, in the open, for a reason given at the
time.

The tally in [GOLDEN_RULES.md](GOLDEN_RULES.md) has nothing to do with any of
this. It counts rule bends, it is generated from the history, and it is a
difficulty setting. Nobody's conduct is scored in it and nothing above ever
touches it.

---

This is a small project and it is written for a room of people who mostly know
each other. If you want the fuller, more formal standard that most projects
adopt, the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org) is the
one, and nothing here is meant to contradict it.
